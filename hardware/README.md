# Hardware

All dimensions are in mm. The assembly frame is Z-up, with Z=0 at the top face of the box lid (see `cad/layout_v3.json`).

## Folders

- `cad/`: generator scripts and assembly data
  - `build_drivetrain.py`: disc, shaft and hub, sealed bushing, spline test coupons
  - `build_disc_v2_chute.py`: chute, plus the legacy disc v2 (superseded by disco_v3, kept for reference)
  - `layout_v3.json`: assembly frame, box size, key heights and positions (notes in Italian)
  - `drivetrain.json`: generated by `build_drivetrain.py` (parameters, hardware list, fit and seal checks)
  - `build_assembly.py`: assembly-frame meshes and `assembly.json` for the 3D viewer on the project site
  - `check_meshes.py`: watertightness check for all exported meshes
- `stl/`: print-ready STLs, already oriented for the bed
- `3mf/`: the same parts at assembled height in the frame above. Rotate them to the print orientation in the slicer.
- `assembly/`: generated by `build_assembly.py` for the 3D viewer, not for printing. Drivetrain parts are in the shaft-axis frame (X=Y=0 on the shaft axis) and the chute is already placed in the assembly frame. `assembly.json` lists every part with its colour, material, spool, status and explode vector, plus the layout numbers used to draw the bought, reused and not-yet-designed parts. A servo cradle 3MF named `*culla*` or `*cradle*` is picked up automatically once it exists.

## Printed parts

### disco_v3: rotating disc

- Purpose: a 103 x 4 mm disc in the bowl with a trapezoid opening. Turning it 180° releases one portion. A 16 mm boss with an 8 mm hex pocket sits on the shaft, and an M3x50 screw through the centre clamps disc, shaft and servo spline together.
- Material: PETG, orange spool. It touches the food.
- Print orientation: top face on the bed, boss up.

### albero_mozzo: shaft and hub

- Purpose: the hub at the bottom has a 25-tooth socket for the servo spline. The 12 mm shaft passes through the bushing, with two grooves for NBR 10x2 O-rings. An 8 mm hex at the top drives the disc.
- Material: PETG, black spool.
- Print orientation: hub on the bed. Use 100 % infill or at least 6 walls (the core is only 9.4 mm at the grooves).

### passante_stagno: sealed bushing

- Purpose: carries the shaft through the lid. A 50 mm flange under the lid holds a 40x2 face O-ring. A 21.5 mm collar rises 4 mm above the lid. The bore is 12.4 mm, and three holes take short M3 heat-set inserts.
- Material: PETG, black spool.
- Print orientation: flange on the bed, so the layer lines run around the bore.

### test_scanalato: spline fit test

- Purpose: three short coupons with the 25-tooth socket at a radial clearance of 0.05, 0.10 and 0.20 mm (marked with 1, 2 and 3 notches). Print them first and try them on the servo. Then set `SPL_CLEAR` in `build_drivetrain.py` and regenerate.
- Material: PETG, black spool (the same spool as the shaft, so the fit carries over).
- Print orientation: as exported, socket on the bed.

### scivolo_v1: chute

- Purpose: a curved hollow duct (2.6 mm wall) that carries the portion out of the bowl. It has a 4 mm bolt-on flange with four 3.4 mm holes.
- Material: PETG, green spool.
- Print orientation: the STL is exported with the flange on top and the outside of the bend on the bed, so it needs supports. Not validated yet; flange-down is an alternative worth testing.

### Servo cradle: not in the repo yet

According to `layout_v3.json`, the servo hangs from the lid in a cradle built into the bushing. It will be printed in PETG from the black spool.

## Bought parts

- 1 x M3x50 DIN912 (disc to shaft to the thread in the servo spline)
- 2 x NBR O-ring 10x2 (shaft)
- 1 x NBR O-ring 40x2 (bushing face seal)
- 3 x M3 heat-set insert, short (4 mm long at most, 4.0 mm hole)
- 3 x M3x10 screw, from above through the bowl floor and the lid into the inserts
- Servo: 25 kg·cm digital servo with a 25-tooth spline (DS3218 / DS3225 class)
- Waterproof box, 265 x 185 mm
- Bowl: a reused part

## Regenerating the parts

```
pip install trimesh manifold3d shapely mapbox_earcut rtree numpy scipy networkx lxml matplotlib
python hardware/cad/build_drivetrain.py
python hardware/cad/build_disc_v2_chute.py
python hardware/cad/build_assembly.py
python hardware/cad/check_meshes.py
```

- Regenerate with `python hardware/cad/build_drivetrain.py`.
- `check_meshes.py` checks that every STL and 3MF is watertight. CI runs it on every push. It rewrites `stl/` and `3mf/` for the drivetrain parts and refreshes `cad/drivetrain.json`.
- `build_disc_v2_chute.py` does the same for the chute.
- `build_assembly.py` reads the 3MF files and `layout_v3.json` and rewrites `assembly/`. Run it after the other two.
- Both scripts work from any folder and write intermediate files to `cad/build/`, which is not committed.
- trimesh needs scipy, networkx and lxml for these scripts, and matplotlib draws the section check image.

## Open points

- The shaft O-rings are squeezed by 25 % with the 9.4 mm groove. The research notes (section 5) suggest about 15 % for a moving seal, for example a 9x2 O-ring in a 9.0 mm groove, and say one O-ring is enough.
- If the printed spline strips at full torque, the research notes (section 2) suggest an aluminium 25-tooth horn screwed to the printed shaft.
- `layout_v3.json` still lists a 44 mm bushing flange. The CAD uses 50 mm so the screws sit inside the face seal.
