    How it works

    Kibble falls from top to bottom. Only the disc moves, and everything electrical stays inside a sealed box.

    1. A container holds the kibble

      Any container will do. It sits above the hopper.

    2. A hopper funnels it into the bowl

      The hopper and its shelf rest on the bowl rim. They are not designed yet.

    3. A disc in the bowl turns 180°

      The disc has a trapezoid opening. When the servo turns it half a revolution, the opening lines up with the spout and one portion drops through.

    4. The chute carries it out

      The portion slides from the bowl spout down the printed chute and into the cat's bowl.

    5. The servo hangs from the lid

      In the design, a 25 kg·cm servo hangs under the box lid in a cradle printed in one piece with the sealed bushing. The shaft passes through the lid with an O-ring, a face O-ring seals the bushing to the lid, and a labyrinth cap on the disc keeps crumbs off the seal.

    6. An ESP32 runs it on low voltage

      A Freenove ESP32-WROVER board runs MicroPython firmware: a power button with a status LED, a dispense button and a short fanfare with every portion. ESPHome, for Home Assistant, is the alternative for later. Prototype 1 runs on a 5 V bench supply; the planned outdoor build takes an external 12 V adapter, so the box only ever sees low voltage.

    Printed parts

    All printed in PETG from three spools. Print the spline test first: it tells you how tightly the shaft will fit your servo.
